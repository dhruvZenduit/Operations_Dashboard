index.html references styles.css and every js/ module by ABSOLUTE URL.
This is not a style preference - MyGeotab renders Add-In markup inside its
own origin, so relative paths would resolve against the MyGeotab host and
404. Classic scripts, not ES modules, for the same reason: cross-origin
modules need CORS headers this host does not send.

If the site moves off zenduit-operations-dashboard.vercel.app, update the
URLs in the <head> of index.html, the two URLs in configuration.json, and
ORIGIN in preview.html.

Load order in <head> is load-bearing (index.html:18-53) and follows the
layers in ARCHITECTURE below: core/ before api/, api/ before services/,
services/ before data-service.js; calc.js and icons.js before ui.js, and
ui.js before every ui/* module, exports.js, the drawer and the four mode
renderers. Each module captures the ones below it at parse time, so a
script tag moved up the list fails on an undefined reference.

DESIGN — ZENDUONE LIGHT
------------------------------------------------------------
The theme is light throughout, built on the ZenduOne reference
stylesheet:

    --navy   #092957   --blue   #1976d2   --good #16845b / #e7f6ef
    --navy-2 #0f3d78   --blue-2 #3b82f6   --warn #b56b05 / #fff4dd
    --ink    #17243b   --line   #e2e8f0   --bad  #c43d3d / #fdeaea
    --muted  #66758d   --radius 12px      page bg #f2f6fb
    font: "Segoe UI", Arial, sans-serif

Brand navy is still the anchor but no longer the surface: white is the
panel, #f2f6fb is the floor, and navy survives as the hero and as the
deep end of the action ramp. The neutrals keep their blue-grey cast. The
ACTION colour is blue - the green is now only "good".

THE SURFACE LADDER. Elevation is carried by a stepped SOLID surface and
a hairline, not by glow. On light the ladder runs out of room, because
nothing is lighter than white, so it has only two real rungs and the
shadows pick up the rest (tailwind.config.js:99-288):

    page       #f2f6fb   the floor
    console    #ffffff   the chrome shell (identity row + control rail)
    (rail      #f5f8fc   the recessed control strip inside it)
    card       #ffffff   panels and popovers
    surface-2  #f5f8fc   the RECESS - inputs, inset rows, sticky heads
    surface-3  #ffffff   LIFTED - the active segment, the toast
    hero       navy      the instrument panel, the one dark object left

Two rungs changed direction in the light pass. surface-2 was the step
ABOVE the card (popovers, drawer head); white has no step above it, so
the elevated things stay white and are lifted by shadow-pop instead, and
surface-2 became the recess an input or an inset row is pushed down
into. surface-3 is white, the same value as the card, and is kept as its
own token because its consumers mean "lifted clear of the track it sits
in" - a distinction the shadow carries here and the colour would carry
again on a flip back.

tests/contrast.test.js asserts the orderings that give a step its
meaning - a floor darker than what sits on it, a recess darker than the
surface it is cut into, and the hero darker than all of them at BOTH
ends of its gradient - so a retuned token cannot quietly flatten the
ladder.

EXACTLY ONE SURFACE GRADIENT, and it is the hero's. `hero-bar` is the
only entry in backgroundImage (tailwind.config.js:367-369), and the
contrast guard asserts the list stays exactly that. `page-glow` (two
radial pools on #occ-root), `console-bar` and `signal-bar` were removed
in the Phase 10 token pass; the console shell and the signal strip are
now the solid `console` and `surface` colours, and the hero's own
::before hairline was flattened to a solid rule. The only other
linear-gradient()s in the built CSS are the two skeleton shimmers
(.occ-sk and .occ-sk-dark, src/styles.src.css:1219 and :1232) -
animation, not surface, and both collapse under prefers-reduced-motion.
There is no radial-gradient anywhere.

IF THE CANONICAL ZENDUIT TOKENS DIFFER FROM THESE, tailwind.config.js is
the only place to change - nothing downstream hardcodes a colour.

THE RAMPS ARE ORDERED BY DISTANCE FROM THE BACKGROUND, not by darkness.
Read this before editing colours. On light that happens to coincide with
the usual reading:

    ink-50 .. ink-300     surfaces, fills, hairlines (light)
    ink-400               decorative / non-text only - fails text
    ink-500 .. ink-900    text, quiet to loud (dark)

The semantic ramps follow the same rule: good-50 is a light tint you put
BEHIND text, good-700 is the dark text you put ON it. Ordering the ramps
this way is what let the theme flip from dark without a single existing
`text-ink-900`, `bg-ink-50` or `border-ink-200` changing meaning.

Two things the flip DID need, and both are structural:
  - `brand` was doing two jobs. On dark it was the green, carrying both
    the action colour (focus rings, the primary button, links) and
    "healthy" (dots, bars, badges). This palette makes those different
    colours, so the green moved out to its own `good` ramp and every
    status consumer was re-pointed at it. `brand` is now purely the
    action blue.
  - THE HERO DID NOT FLIP. It is still a navy gradient, so it is the one
    surface where the ramps still run the other way round: its condition
    pill, its lit lamps, its meter fill and its action link take the
    -200/-300 step, scoped under .occ-instrument. The lamp tokens
    themselves are mid-tones so they read on a white card, and a
    mid-tone on navy measures 1.7:1 - which is exactly why the hero
    needs its own step, and why a guard pair asserts that failure.

WHITE, NOT NAVY, ON THE BLUE FILLS. `surface` IS white on this theme,
which is what lets the primary button, the active tab and the brandmark
carry white text with no colour of their own - 5.8:1 on brand-600 and
4.6:1 on the -500 hover. The dark theme could not: its green fills
measured white at 2.91:1 and had to set navy instead.

ACCESSIBILITY. 74 text/background pairs are measured against WCAG AA by
tests/contrast.test.js: 54 held to 4.5:1 as text, 20 to 3:1 as non-text
or large readouts, plus 6 pairs that must KEEP failing - good-500 on its
own tint, amber-500 as text on a card, the reference stylesheet's own
--warn on its tint, a mid-tone lamp on the hero, and ink-400 on a card
and on the page. The guard requires tailwind.config.js itself, so
changing a colour re-measures every pair it appears in. Status is never
carried by colour alone: every state pairs a tint with an icon and a
written label.

Two contrast exceptions are deliberate and are NOT in the guard:
  - ink-300 as the 3px severity edge on the NEUTRAL rail (.occ-rail
    .is-info and its worklist and queue siblings) measures 1.47:1 on a
    card. It marks the ABSENCE of severity, and every such row states its
    severity in a written chip, so 1.4.11 is met by the chip. Raising it
    would re-space the ramp for the other border-ink-300 users.
  - .occ-nodata-ghostval is ink-300 (1.47:1) - the em-dash placeholder,
    already aria-hidden.

  (The third exception the dark theme carried - ink-500 on surface-3 at
  4.37:1 - is gone: surface-3 is white now, so the whole text ramp is
  legible on it and the pair is measured rather than excused.)

DESIGN — READOUTS AND THE INSTRUMENT PANEL
------------------------------------------------------------
The visual language is borrowed from the world the dashboard describes:
gauge clusters, scan tools, indicator lamps, stencilled unit numbers.
Two decisions carry it.

1. DATA IS SET IN MONO. Every readout - percentages, odometers, engine
   hours, durations, fault codes, counts - uses the mono stack, while
   labels and prose stay sans. That contrast is what makes the page read
   as an instrument rather than a web page, and it aligns numeric columns
   for free. Dates that contain a month name stay in sans (mono makes
   "Aug 21, 2026" clunky); the rule is mono for digits and codes.

2. THE HERO IS AN INSTRUMENT PANEL. Fleet Health sits on the lightest
   surface on the page, with a lamp cluster, and it is the only panel
   allowed a real shadow and the only one allowed a gradient. Everything
   else is a solid card on a hairline. Previously every panel looked
   equally important; now there is one unambiguous anchor.

Supporting choices: hairline-led panels rather than shadowed cards,
tighter radii (8px panels, 12px hero) so it reads as equipment rather
than a friendly app, uppercase wide-tracked labels for eyebrows and
column headers, a squarer meter instead of a pill progress bar, and a
severity rail on the leading cell of each Attention row - which encodes
the sort the list is already in.

Type scale: 54px hero number, 13px section titles in sentence case, 10px
tracked uppercase eyebrows, ink-500 for muted metadata.

No webfonts. An Add-In runs inside MyGeotab's page, where an external
font host is one more thing a CSP can block, so both stacks resolve to
faces already on the machine (Cascadia Mono / Consolas on Windows, SF
Mono on macOS).

RESPONSIVE LADDER. Three steps, not five (src/styles.src.css:113-146):

    phone     < 640px    single column; the drawer is a full-width sheet
    compact   < 1024px   the dense row grids give up their columns
    full      >= 1180px  the asymmetric hero grid, the even pair grid
                         and the 4/5/6-up metric rail

`screens.lg` is repointed to 1180px (tailwind.config.js:80-82), so every
`lg:` utility lands on the desktop step. That closes the old 1024-1179px
dead band, where the pair grids went two-up while the hero was still
stacked. Above 1680px the page stops growing and centres. Checked at
1920 / 1440 / 1280 / 1024 / 820 / 390.

MOTION. The live ring is the only loop. Skeleton shimmer, the 150ms
pop-ins and the refresh state machine are the only other motion, and all
of it is dropped under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`.

------------------------------------------------------------
STYLING / BUILD
------------------------------------------------------------
Styling is Tailwind CSS, compiled ahead of time:

   npm install          once
   npm run build:css    one-shot build
   npm run watch:css    rebuild on save while editing
   npm test             the test suite (see TESTS)

Edit src/styles.src.css. Never edit styles.css — it is generated and
overwritten on every build. It IS committed, so deploying stays a plain
static file copy with no build step on Vercel.

Three settings in tailwind.config.js are load-bearing for MyGeotab, not
preferences:

  preflight: false     Preflight is a GLOBAL element reset. An Add-In
                       renders inside MyGeotab's own page, so a global
                       reset would restyle MyGeotab's chrome around us.
                       The resets we do need are scoped under #occ-root
                       in the @layer base block of the source file.

  important: "#occ-root"
                       Emits every utility as `#occ-root .foo`. Nothing
                       leaks out of the Add-In, and the extra specificity
                       beats MyGeotab's framework styles on bare
                       table/button/input/h1 tags without !important.

  container: false     `container` is the one utility Tailwind emits
                       UNSCOPED even with `important` set, so it would
                       escape into MyGeotab — where other stylesheets on
                       the page are very likely to use `.container` too.

The Tailwind CDN script is NOT an option here: it injects styles into a
runtime <style> tag, and MyGeotab strips <style> from <head>.

Tailwind finds class names by scanning the `content` glob
(tailwind.config.js:40-45): index.html, preview.html, script.js and
./js/**/*.js. The ** matters - js/core, js/api, js/services, js/ui and
js/mock all live one level down, and a single-star glob would drop their
utilities from the build. A class assembled at runtime ("bg-" + tone) is
invisible to that scan too, so the modules keep complete class strings in
lookup maps instead.

THE FOUR MODES
------------------------------------------------------------
The dashboard is built around SEE -> UNDERSTAND -> INVESTIGATE -> ACT.
Four tabs over one shared context (index.html:132-153).

OVERVIEW      The landing view, and the only one designed to be read in
              three seconds.
                Fleet Health        one number plus the breakdown
                Telemetry           which feeds are live, and which are not
                Attention Required  the ranked worklist
                Site Performance    compare locations
                Fleet Activity      is this getting better or worse?
                Asset Health        distribution across the fleet
                Camera Health       coverage, or an honest empty state

OPERATIONS    The detail behind Overview, in the order an operator works
              it:
                Fleet Health        the compact KPI rail
                Attention Required  the same worklist
                Plan vs Actual      dispatch routes against what the
                                    vehicle actually did
                Site Performance    per-site totals
                Fleet Activity      fault history
                DVIR Compliance     expected / completed / overdue by site
                Asset Health        distribution
                Assets Offline      OFFLINE only, never Quiet
                Cameras not working, Cameras not recording
                Driver scorecard    a link card into Safety

MAINTENANCE   Fleet condition, and the only write action in the product.
                Maintenance Health  hero plus six tiles - Open work orders
                                    (Not available), Critical faults,
                                    Overdue maintenance (Not available),
                                    Due soon (Not available), DVIR
                                    defects, Vehicles down
                Critical Maintenance  the table an operator acts from:
                                    Vehicle, Fault, Duration, Status, and
                                    Investigate / Create work order
                Fault board, Inspection defects, Asset register
                Export maintenance data   the dialog described in EXPORTS

SAFETY        Loaded the first time the tab is opened.
                Hero                fleet score, events, drivers requiring
                                    attention and critical events, each
                                    with "vs previous period"
                Driver scorecard    Rank, Driver, Score, Events, Trend,
                                    Status; a row opens the driver drawer
                Safety events       a [Driver] [Vehicle] toggle and a
                                    category breakdown showing only the
                                    categories that exist in the account

ATTENTION REQUIRED is ranked by js/core/rank.js, not by age. One row per
asset - an asset with a critical fault AND an outage is one problem to
the person who has to call someone - carrying every open issue worst
first with a "+N more" chip. The sort tuple is

    1. severity        critical > warning > info
    2. impact          concurrent issues, plus whatever the caller adds
    3. duration open   longest first
    4. recency         most recently changed first

Severity comes from the data's own semantics, never from age alone
(rank.js:57-77, plus the DVIR issues script.js injects):

    CRITICAL  an active fault marked Critical, or a red stop lamp
    CRITICAL  the latest DVIR says the vehicle is not safe to operate
    CRITICAL  OFFLINE (over 72 h) on an asset that ALSO carries a
              critical fault — the only way an outage is critical
    WARNING   OFFLINE (over 72 h) otherwise
    WARNING   an active warning fault
    WARNING   an open DVIR defect with no repair recorded
    INFO      a missed inspection

An asset whose findings are ALL informational gets no row of its own
(rank.js:122) - a worklist of sixty "overdue DVIR" lines is a list nobody
reads; those findings ride along on assets that already need attention.
QUIET, UNKNOWN and NOT LINKED assets NEVER appear here: they are stated
on Fleet Health's meta-line and reachable through the status filter. The
list is capped at 8 rows (rank.js:35) with the real total in the badge
and a Show all expansion.

Unconnected feeds (camera, dispatch) appear as their own panels with a
"Not connected" state, never inside the worklist. They are real gaps, but
nothing an operator does today clears them, so counting them as open
issues would permanently inflate the badge and mean "All clear" could
never show.

DETAIL DRAWER  Clicking any asset row - in Attention Required, Assets
              Offline, DVIR, Critical Maintenance or the register - opens
              a right-side drawer. It is a router with four kinds
              (drawer.js:918-946):
                asset     status, communication, meters, per-signal
                          diagnostics, location, a merged timeline and
                          the active faults
                incident  the issue that was clicked FIRST, then the
                          asset body, so the drawer can never name a
                          different event than the row that opened it
                driver    one driver's score, events and trend
                route     one dispatch route, its stop table and its strip
              Filters, scroll position and mode all survive, so you can
              work down the worklist without re-orienting. Escape closes
              it and returns focus.

FLEET ACTIVITY plots newly detected faults from FaultData.firstDetected
across the 30-day window the live path already fetches (calc.js:616-624)
- real history, not a synthesised trend. Historical active/offline is
deliberately NOT plotted: DeviceStatusInfo reports only the CURRENT state
per device, so an availability trend would have to be invented.

COMMUNICATION STATUS
------------------------------------------------------------
js/core/status.js is THE definition. Every count, badge, filter and
worklist rule reads it; nothing else may decide what "offline" means.
Five states (status.js:26-32), decided against the one timestamp that
means anything - DeviceStatusInfo.dateTime - in this order:

  NOT_LINKED    The Device record has deviceType "None", an empty serial,
  "Not linked"  or a serial of all zeros (status.js:90-98). There is no
                telematics device behind the asset, so it CANNOT report
                and must never be counted as offline. Third-party
                CustomDevice / CustomVehicleDevice hardware does report,
                and counts as linked.

  UNKNOWN       Linked, but MyGeotab has never received anything: no
  "Unknown"     DeviceStatusInfo row at all, an unparseable date, or a
                sentinel date before 1990-01-01 (status.js:71) - MyGeotab
                writes 1986-01-01, and occasionally 0001-01-01, to mean
                "never".

  OFFLINE       Silent for MORE THAN 72 HOURS (status.js:144-147).
  "Offline"     "Not communicating."

  RECENTLY_OFFLINE  Silent for longer than the quiet threshold but within
  "Quiet"       72 h (status.js:148-151). Flagged, NOT counted as offline.

  CONNECTED     Heard from within the quiet threshold.
  "Connected"

WHY A DEVICE WITH NO DeviceStatusInfo ROW IS UNKNOWN, NOT OFFLINE. Saying
"offline" asserts a fact - this asset went quiet - that no reading
supports. Nothing was ever received, so there is no last-seen to measure
from. The previous version fell back to Device.activeFrom, which for an
unlinked or never-reporting device is often 1986-01-01, and reported a
14,000-day outage; that fallback is why the dashboard used to claim "950
offline". lastSeen now comes only from DeviceStatusInfo.dateTime, and an
unusable value becomes null, which becomes UNKNOWN - never "active".

TWO THRESHOLDS, ONE OF THEM TUNABLE (status.js:64-67):

  quietAfterMinutes  240 (4 hours) by default. This is the control in the
                     filter panel, labelled "Quiet after" - 1 h / 4 h /
                     12 h / 24 h (filters.js:135-141). Changing it
                     re-decorates the assets already loaded; no refetch.
  offlineAfterHours  72. A fixed business rule and a constant in the
                     file, not a setting. If quiet is ever configured at
                     or past 72 h it falls back to the default, because
                     quiet can never begin after offline does
                     (status.js:107-109).

getCommunicationStatus returns { state, ageMs, ageMinutes, reason,
policy }, and the reason string is what the drawer prints: "Last
communication 14d 0h ago — over the 72-hour threshold."
isDeviceCommunicating is stored and shown in the drawer but does NOT
drive classification: its window is undocumented, while the age rule is
deterministic and explainable.

FLEET HEALTH ARITHMETIC (status.js:178-196). One function, so the hero,
the KPI rail and the site table cannot disagree:

    total          every asset in the current context
    notLinked      NOT_LINKED
    linked         total minus notLinked
    reporting      CONNECTED
    quiet          RECENTLY_OFFLINE
    offline        OFFLINE
    communicating  CONNECTED + RECENTLY_OFFLINE

    Operational %  = communicating / linked          (status.js:193)
                     "heard from within 72 hours, of the assets that can
                     report at all". Not-linked assets are excluded from
                     the denominator: an asset with no device cannot fail
                     a communication test, and counting it as a failure
                     would make the headline a measure of procurement.
    Reporting %    = CONNECTED / total               (status.js:194)

The hero's meta-line states "N quiet within 72 h, N not linked, N
unknown" so none of those three can hide inside a zero, and the status
filter is a five-way choice over the same states.

GROUP CONTEXT
------------------------------------------------------------
The group tree is the primary context, not a control buried in a panel.

THE PICKER is a tree popover in the console rail (js/ui/group-picker.js)
showing the breadcrumb of the current selection and its asset count. It
is role="tree" with full keyboard operation (arrows to move and to
collapse or expand, Enter to select, Home/End, type-ahead), a search box,
right-aligned mono counts per node, a partial-selection dot on the
ancestors of a selected child, and Clear. Escape and an outside click
close it.

DESCENDANT CLOSURE. Selecting a node selects every asset in it and in all
of its children, all the way down. The closure is a memoised Set built
once per load (js/core/group-tree.js), so selecting a root costs the same
as selecting a leaf.

MULTI-GROUP MEMBERSHIP. An asset carries every direct membership, not
just the deepest one. A device that belongs to both Lampson/Trucks and
Service is found under either. The old single groupId hid multi-group
devices under a sibling branch.

UNGROUPED (N) is an explicit node. Assets with no group used to vanish
from every selection that was not "All groups"; now they are visible and
selectable.

SEEDED FROM MYGEOTAB. On initialize and on focus the Add-In reads
MyGeotab's own group filter (geotab-api.js:69-73, script.js:208-212) and
uses it as the starting selection, so the Add-In respects the shell
instead of competing with it; the in-page picker refines it. Group ids
left over from a different database are dropped on load rather than
filtering everything to nothing with no way back.

SITE IS REDEFINED. A site is no longer a copy of the group name. It is
THE CHILD OF THE SELECTED NODE THAT CONTAINS THE ASSET
(group-tree.js:186-212): under "All groups" the sites are the roots;
under one node, its children; under several nodes, the nodes themselves;
an asset attached directly to the selected node reports that node. This
is what makes Site Performance and DVIR-by-site change meaningfully as
you move up and down the tree. The separate Site filter is gone, because
it duplicated the group dimension.

Every mode, the drawer and every export read the same OCC.context object,
so a divergent per-module context is structurally impossible.

ARCHITECTURE
------------------------------------------------------------
  js/api/         geotab-api.js  ·  zenduone-api.js
   |
   +- js/core/    pure, node-testable: requests · cache · status ·
   |              group-tree · context · rank · route-metrics ·
   |              dvir-metrics · safety-metrics
      |
      +- js/services/  camera · trip · route · dvir · maintenance · safety
         |
         +- js/data-service.js   the mock/live seam and the fleet model
            |
            +- js/calc.js        KPI derivations
               |
               +- js/ui.js + js/ui/*  primitives, hero, worklist,
               |                      route-strip, group-picker, modal,
               |                      dialogs, and js/drawer.js
                  |
                  +- modes  overview.js · operations.js ·
                     |      maintenance.js · safety.js
                     |
                     +- script.js  the shell: lifecycle, context,
                                   delegated events

Each layer knows only the ones below it. js/core/* is pure - data in,
data out, no DOM and no API - so every business rule can be reasoned
about and tested on its own; each core module attaches to window.OCC and
also sets module.exports when `module` exists, which is how `node --test`
loads it with no build step and no bundler. data-service.js is still the
seam: it decides mock versus live and normalises both onto one asset
shape, so nothing above it contains a branch on where the data came from.

THREE CONSTRAINTS THE LAYOUT MUST RESPECT:

  Load order    The <script> tags in index.html:18-53 are the module
                system. Their order follows the layers above, and each
                module captures the ones below it at parse time.
  Absolute URLs Every tag carries the full Vercel prefix, because a
                relative path resolves against MyGeotab's host.
  Tailwind glob tailwind.config.js:40-45 must keep `./js/**/*.js`. Under
                a single-star glob the class names in js/core, js/ui,
                js/services and js/mock are invisible to the build and
                their utilities are silently dropped.

There is one global, window.OCC. No runtime <style>, no external font or
CDN, no ES modules.

INSTALL
------------------------------------------------------------
The Add-In is hosted at https://zenduit-operations-dashboard.vercel.app
and configuration.json points at it, so there are no files to upload.

1. In MyGeotab, go to Administration > System > System Settings > Add-Ins.
2. Turn on "Allow unsigned Add-Ins".
3. Click New Add-In, open the Configuration tab, and paste the contents
   of configuration.json.

PREVIEW WITHOUT MYGEOTAB

   http://localhost:8000/preview.html

preview.html reads index.html, rewrites the absolute asset URLs to

With no MyGeotab session the Add-In loads sample data and shows a
"Preview mode" banner, so nobody mistakes the sample numbers for real
ones. The source is decided at call time from whether MyGeotab handed the

ZenduONE call is ever made in preview: a real network request standing

PREVIEW DATA IS SAMPLE DATA. js/mock/fixtures.js supplies the preview
fleet plus DVIR logs, trips, dispatch jobs, exception events and rules.
It is shaped to exercise the product, not to model a customer: the
lastSeen distribution deliberately contains assets in all five
communication states, and the safety profiles are tuned so the driver
scorecard occupies all four bands, Excellent through High risk, with a
non-zero "drivers requiring attention" count and one Unassigned-driver
row scoring null. tests/safety-fixtures.test.js locks that shape, so the
review surface cannot quietly go all-green again. None of it touches or
resembles live data.

EXTERNAL FEEDS
------------------------------------------------------------
CAMERA HEALTH IS WIRED. ZenduONE's DeviceStatusInfo carries a
cameraStatus string per device, and MyGeotab has no camera concept at
all, so this is THE source. ZenduONE devices are joined to MyGeotab
assets by serialNumber, which both sides expose. One fetch per load
(Device plus DeviceStatusInfo, both cached), then counted over the assets
currently on screen so the panels follow the group context.

The vocabulary of cameraStatus beyond "offline" is not documented, so
js/services/camera-service.js:31-42 maps the known words and refuses to
guess at the rest:

  active         online, ok, recording, connected, active, normal,
                 healthy, up
  not recording  not recording, no recording, idle, no video, no clip,
                 storage, sd card
  not working    offline, unreachable, disconnected, down, no signal,
                 error, fault
  no camera      the field is absent or empty - the device simply has no
                 camera, and is not counted at all
  unknown        any other string. Reported as Unknown, and never counted
                 as either healthy or broken.

DRIVER SCORING IS WIRED, AND DERIVED. There is no vendor score in either
API; js/core/safety-metrics.js computes one from ExceptionEvents and
Trips (formula, weights and bands under WHERE THE NUMBERS COME FROM), and
every score in the UI carries a "Derived" badge. It lives on the Safety
tab; Operations keeps a link card into it.

STILL NOT AVAILABLE FROM THE CURRENT APIs, and rendered as an explicit
"Not available" rather than a zero or a dash:

  Open work orders      ZenduONE Maintenance is write-only in the
                        published collection (PLAN.md section 3, row 16).
  Overdue maintenance   No schedule or reminder source exists in either
  Due soon              API (PLAN.md section 3, row 17).
  Vendor safety scores  Neither API returns one (row 21).
  XLSX export           Not without a library of roughly 400 KB (row 19).

The one WRITE the product performs is ZenduONE CreateRepairRequest, from
"Create work order" in the drawer and in the Critical Maintenance table.
It is disabled with a stated reason when there is no ZenduONE session or
no serial match for the asset. Because there is no read side, a request
raised this way cannot be listed back into the dashboard: the success
toast carries its service number, and that is the receipt.

CACHING AND REFRESH
------------------------------------------------------------
ONE TTL TABLE SERVES BOTH APIs (js/core/cache.js:21-30). The values are
chosen against how fast each thing actually changes, not how often we
would like to poll - a group tree changes weekly, a device's
communication state by the minute:

    group     30 min   MyGeotab Group
    device    10 min   MyGeotab Device, ZenduONE Device
    status     60 s    DeviceStatusInfo, and ZenduONE's (cameraStatus)
    fault      5 min   FaultData
    window     5 min   DVIRLog / Trip / ExceptionEvent, per day
    job        2 min   ZenduONE Job
    rule       60 min  MyGeotab Rule, ZenduONE Rule
    user       30 min  MyGeotab User, ZenduONE User

There is no separate ZenduONE table: a ZenduONE read takes the key that
matches what it returns, so zenduone:status uses status, zenduone:jobs
uses job, zenduone:devices uses device, and so on. Two lifetimes live
outside the table. Trips for a PAST day are held 30 minutes
(trip-service.js:20) because a closed day cannot change, while today's
trips take the 60-second status TTL; and a reverse-geocoded address is
held one hour per rounded coordinate (drawer.js:100).

DE-DUPLICATION. getOrFetch keeps an in-flight map: two callers asking for
the same key while a fetch is running receive the SAME promise, not two
requests. Errors are never cached.

RETRY (js/core/requests.js:84-100). Up to 3 attempts with 1 s then 3 s
of back-off, and ONLY on transient failures - network errors, timeouts,
5xx and MyGeotab OverLimit. Authentication and permission failures are
final: retrying them would repeat the refusal and burn the rate budget.

LOAD GENERATION. api.call has no abort path, so a load that has been
superseded cannot be cancelled. Every load carries a generation number
instead, and a late response from an older generation is dropped rather
than painted over newer data.

STALE DATA. A failed REFRESH keeps the last good numbers on screen and
says how old they are: an amber banner reading "Latest refresh failed —
<reason> Showing data from HH:MM" with a Try again button
(script.js:487-491). The full-page "Unable to load fleet data" card
appears only when there is no prior good data to keep.

REFRESH IS MANUAL. The only timer in the codebase is the one-second clock
in the header (script.js:437), cleared on blur. There is no auto-refresh
poll; MyGeotab's own focus event triggers a reload when the tab is
returned to.

WHAT LOADS WHEN (script.js:85-146). On the initial load, in one chain:
the fleet, the ZenduONE session, camera health, the ZenduONE device
warm-up that answers the serial join behind "Create work order", and then
routes and DVIR before the panels are built.

  Routes and DVIR are EAGER, not lazy. They feed the Plan vs Actual and
  DVIR Compliance panels AND the Attention Required worklist, all of
  which are on the landing view, so deferring them would paint
  placeholders into the first screen.

  SAFETY is the one lazy load (script.js:566). Thirty days of trips and
  exception events is the heaviest read in the app, and nothing outside
  the Safety tab depends on it, so it is fetched the first time that tab
  is opened - and re-fetched after a refresh only if it had been opened.

None of routes, DVIR or safety is ever on the critical path: a failure in
any of them becomes an "unavailable" panel, never a blank dashboard.
